You are not selling software.
You are selling a salon owner their own money back.

Read this once before your first meeting. Read the scripts twice. Everything here is written the way you should actually say it — not the way a brochure says it.

Every salon loses money to two things nobody has time to fix: customers who don't turn up, and happy customers who never leave a review. Both are boring, repetitive jobs. Rebound does both, automatically, on WhatsApp. That's the whole product.
01

What is Rebound?

learn this cold — you'll say it 40 times a week

If someone stops you in the street and asks, this is the answer. Twenty seconds, no jargon, no technical words.

Say exactly this
"Rebound is a WhatsApp number for your salon. You text it your bookings — like you'd text a friend. It reminds your customers so they don't forget and don't skip. After they visit, it asks the happy ones to leave you a Google review. And on the 1st of every month it sends you one message telling you how much money it saved you. There's no app to download. There's nothing to learn. If you can use WhatsApp, you already know how to use it."
Why this works: it never uses the words software, platform, dashboard, CRM, automation or AI. The moment you use one of those, the owner thinks "this is going to be complicated and I'll have to learn it" — and you have lost them. Salon owners don't fear the price. They fear the effort.

The five things it actually does

🔔
Reminds customers before they come
One reminder the day before, one a couple of hours before. Fewer people forget. Fewer empty chairs.
⭐
Asks happy customers for a Google review
Automatically, after the visit, in the salon's own name. This is the part that quietly grows their business.
🛟
Catches the unhappy customer before Google does
It asks privately first. Angry ones get routed to the owner instead of to a public 1-star review.
↩️
Brings customers back
Haircut after 3 weeks, facial after 6. A gentle nudge at the right gap — the thing every salon means to do and never does.
🎁
Wins back the ones who drifted away
If a regular stops coming, Rebound sends them services worth ₹200–500 free on their next visit. You only spend money on customers you were about to lose.

The meeting — the full script

ten minutes, in this order, don't skip step 2

Walk in with a phone, a pen and one sheet of paper. Nothing else. No laptop, no printed brochure, no folder — the moment you open a folder it becomes a sales meeting and their guard goes up.

Step 1Walk in — don't pitch yet (30 seconds)
"Hi, Rahul — we spoke on the phone. Nice place, how long have you been here?"
↳ Look around. Notice something real — the chairs, how busy it is, a photo on the wall. Salon owners are proud of their place and almost nobody who walks in to sell them something acts like a human first.
Read the room before you speak. If there's a customer in the chair, say: "You're busy — I'll wait, or shall I come back at 3?" Offering to leave is the fastest way to be asked to stay.
Step 2Ask, then shut up (2 minutes) — the most important step
Ask in this order
"Roughly how many customers do you do in a month?"

"And in a normal week, how many book and just don't turn up?"

"What's an average bill here?"

"Who reminds them — do you, or does someone at the counter?"

"And have you ever had a bad review you only found out about days later?"
↳ Write every answer down. Say nothing except "hmm" and "right". This is 80% of the sale and you have not pitched anything yet.
Why this works: by the time you speak, they've told you their problem in their own words. Now you're not selling — you're answering the thing they just said. Completely different conversation.
Step 3Show, don't tell (1 minute) — this closes more deals than anything else
"Let me just show you what it is — it's easier than explaining."
↳ Take out your phone. Send a booking into the demo number right in front of them: "Priya 98200 12345 haircut kal 4 baje". Hold the phone where they can see. Wait for the reply.
"That's it. That's the whole thing. That's what your girl at the counter does — types it like a text. Everything after that happens on its own."
Hand them your phone and let them type one. The moment they do it themselves, "I'll have to learn software" dies permanently. Nothing else you say all meeting is as powerful as this.
Step 4Do the maths on paper (2 minutes)
"Can I use this?" (their pen, their paper if possible)

"You said 8 no-shows a week, ₹700 a bill. So that's ₹5,600 a week walking out. ₹22,400 a month. Reminders usually cut that about in half — so call it ₹11,000 a month coming back to you."

"It costs ₹7,999 a month."
↳ Slide the paper across. SAY NOTHING. Let them read it. The first person to speak loses.
Then, once they've reacted
"And that's before a single review. The reviews, the comeback reminders, catching the unhappy ones — all of that is on top."
Step 5The price and the setup fee (1 minute)
"So it's ₹7,999 a month, and ₹9,999 once at the start to set it up."
↳ Stop. Do not explain the setup fee unless they ask. Half of them won't.
If they flinch at ₹9,999
"That's a week of work that only happens once — we build you your own WhatsApp business number in your salon's name, get every message approved by Meta, connect your Google, load your menu, and move your diary in. And it's exactly why there's no contract. We've been paid for the build, so you can walk away any month. The ones who don't charge upfront tie you in for a year instead."
Step 6Close on the calendar, not the contract (1 minute)
"I've got Thursday morning free for setup. Shall I hold it for you?"
↳ It's far easier to say yes to a time slot than to a signature. You're not asking them to commit to a product — you're asking about their Thursday.
Then, immediately
"Setup's ₹9,999 — shall we do it on UPI now so I can lock Thursday in?"
Collect the money in the room. A payment promised for tomorrow is a payment that never happens. If they truly can't pay now, get a date and a time in your phone in front of them, and send a WhatsApp confirming it before you're out of the door.

What to talk about — and what to never bring up

✓ Talk about this
  • Their no-show number — always their number, never yours
  • Money walking out the door every week
  • Their bad review story — ask, then listen
  • How little work it is: one tap
  • "There's no app, nothing to learn"
  • "There's no contract, leave any month"
  • Their staff being too busy to remember
  • The demo on your phone — show it early
  • Their Google rating and how it can move
✕ Never bring up
  • The words software, platform, dashboard, CRM, AI, automation
  • How it works technically — nobody cares, and it scares them
  • Other clients' names or their numbers
  • Competitors — never criticise another product by name
  • Discounts — not even hinting one might exist
  • Anything you're not sure about — say "I'll find out and text you today"
  • Long feature lists — three benefits beat ten features
  • Your commission or how the agent scheme works
  • Promises about exact results — say "typically", never "guaranteed"

An objection is not a no. It's usually the first honest thing they've said. Answer one properly rather than three quickly — and always ask which objection is the real one.

₹"It's too expensive."›
"Let's check that against your own numbers." — then do the maths out loud, on paper. "So the question isn't whether ₹7,999 is a lot. It's whether ₹11,000 coming back is worth ₹7,999 going out. And the reviews are on top of that, free."
Don't defend the price or compare to other products. The moment you justify, you've agreed it's expensive. Go straight to their no-show number.
₹"₹9,999 upfront is too much — remove it."›
"I can't remove it, but let me tell you what it buys — it's not profit, it's a week of work." Run the list: your own verified WhatsApp number in your salon's name, every message approved by Meta, Google connected, menu loaded, diary moved in. "And it's exactly why there's no contract. We've been paid for the build, so you're free to leave any month. The ones who don't charge upfront lock you in for a year instead — ask them."
If they still won't: offer to split it — ₹5,000 now, ₹4,999 on day 30. Never zero. A free setup gets treated like a free thing.
!"We already send reminders."›
"Good — then you already believe in it, which makes this easy. Who sends them?" (Reception.) "And on the days she's busy, or off, or it's a Saturday rush?"
↳ Pause here. Let them answer. They know.
"That's the gap. And the bigger one: does she ask every happy customer for a Google review? Does she catch the unhappy one before they post? Does she tell you on the 1st how much money it saved you? That's the part nobody does by hand."
This is a buying signal, not a rejection. They've already accepted the premise. Slow down here — don't rush past it.
!"I don't have time to learn new software."›
"There's nothing to learn — that's the whole point. No app, no login, no dashboard. You text us the booking like you'd text your sister. After the appointment you tap one green tick. That's it. If you can use WhatsApp, you're already trained."
Then show them. Open WhatsApp and send a booking into the demo number right there. The reply comes back in five seconds. This closes more deals than anything else in this playbook.
!"We tried something like this. It didn't work."›
"What happened?"
↳ Listen properly. It's almost always: staff stopped updating it.
"Right — and that's exactly why they fail. Every one of them needs somebody to open software and type things in. After two weeks nobody does. Ours needs one tap on WhatsApp, on the phone that's already in your hand. And if your team stops tapping, we see it from our side within a day and I call you."
Ask which one they tried. If it was a booking app or billing system: "That's a different thing — that's software for you to run. This is a service that runs for you."
!"My customers will get irritated by messages."›
"Two reminders before their appointment, and one message after. That's it — not marketing blasts, not offers. Nobody has ever been annoyed at being reminded about their own appointment; they get annoyed when they forget and lose the slot. And every message has a STOP option — anyone who wants out is out instantly and permanently."
If they're worried about their premium customers specifically: messages go out in the salon's own name, written properly. Show them a real one on your phone.
?"Let me think about it / I'll ask my partner."›
"Of course — it's your money. Can I ask one thing though: is it the price, or is it whether it'll actually work?"
↳ Wait. The real objection comes out here. Answer that one, not the polite one.
"Tell you what. Let me set up the reminders this week. By Friday you'll see confirmations landing on your own phone from your own customers. If it isn't obviously working by day 30, cancel — there's no contract, and we hand you your customer list on the way out."
Never leave without a next date. If they genuinely need the partner: "When are you both around? I'll come back then — ten minutes, both of you." Put it in your phone in front of them.
₹"Give me a discount."›
"On the monthly I can't — and honestly you wouldn't want me to. Everyone pays the same, and you'd be pretty annoyed later to find the salon down the road got it cheaper. What I can do is the annual: pay for the year upfront and two months are free. On Growth that's ₹16,000 back in your pocket."
Hold this line. A client who negotiates once will negotiate every quarter. Annual is the only lever — and it's the better outcome for you anyway.
?"What if it doesn't work for us?"›
"Then you cancel. No contract, no notice period, no penalty. And we export your full customer list and review history and hand it to you — it's your data, we're only holding it. The only thing you'd have spent is the setup, and that's already bought you a verified WhatsApp business number in your own name that stays yours."
This is your strongest card. Most competitors can't say it. Use it early rather than saving it for the end.
?"After Diwali. / Next month. / After the season."›
"Fair enough. Let me just put a number on the wait." — use their own figures — "At 8 no-shows a week, two months of waiting is about ₹45,000 gone. Setup takes a week, so if we start now you're live before the rush. And the rush is exactly when a no-show hurts most, because that slot could have been sold twice over."
If they still won't move: get the date in writing and book the follow-up before you leave. A vague "next month" is a no wearing a polite face.
!"My salon is small, I don't need all this."›
"How many appointments in a week?"
↳ Under 25 a week — walk away politely. The maths won't hold and they'll cancel by month three.
If it's 40+ a week, it does hold
"Then it's not small in the way that matters. And honestly, small is where a no-show hurts most. When a big chain loses a slot they've got twenty more that day. When you lose one, that's your whole afternoon gone."
Walking away is a skill, not a failure. You're paid on clients who stay, not clients who sign. A bad-fit client costs you the bonus and two months of your time.
?"Can I just take the reviews part?"›
"The review part only works because of the rest. We only ask for a review after you've told us the customer actually turned up and was happy — that's what makes it land instead of feeling like spam. Without the appointment side it's just another blasting tool. Those exist for ₹999 a month, and they're the reason people ignore review requests now."
Same answer for "can I just take the reminders?" The pieces are worth far more together.
!"Won't people just wait for the free offer instead of booking?"›
"Fair question, and it's why it isn't a fixed schedule. It doesn't go out every time someone's a bit late — there's a long gap before anyone can get another one, and if someone ignores two we stop sending. Nobody can sit and wait for it. And honestly, the person gaming a ₹400 voucher every four months is still coming to you four times a year instead of going somewhere else."
You control the dial. If an owner is nervous, start them at ₹200 and a longer wait. They can raise it once they've seen it work — and they change it themselves by texting COUPON.
?"Is my customer data safe? Who sees it?"›
"Only you. We hold it for you, we never sell it, and we never message your customers about anything except their own appointments with you. No offers, no ads, nothing from us. And the day you leave, we export the whole list and hand it over."
Rare objection, but the owners who ask it are usually the serious, higher-value ones. Answer plainly and don't get flustered.

What they have to give us

say this BEFORE they pay — surprises after payment cause refunds

Go through this list on the closing call. Five minutes here saves you a wasted week and an angry client. The three yellow ones are the ones that actually block go-live.

📱
A phone number that has never had WhatsApp on it
This is the one that trips everybody up, so say it clearly and early. A brand-new SIM (₹200 from any shop) or an old spare number is fine — but if WhatsApp is on it now, or ever was and wasn't removed properly, Meta will reject it. The salon keeps this number — it becomes their business line forever.
If they say "I'll use my personal number" — no. That number already has WhatsApp. Ask them to buy a fresh SIM before setup day. This one line saves you a wasted week.

We email a link. The owner opens it, signs in with the Google account that manages their business listing, clicks Allow. Sixty seconds of clicking — but they need to know which Google account it is. Half the time the login sits with a cousin or an old agency. Ask on the call: "who set up your Google listing?"
💇
Service menu with prices

This week's bookings and the staff's phone numbers

📊

This is the baseline. Without it the monthly "we saved you ₹X" message has nothing to measure against. A rough guess is fine — just get a number written down.
🖼
Their logo
Any decent image. It goes on the monthly summary.

How long it takes, and the day it goes live

promise 7 working days — never promise faster

Walk them through this on the closing call so nothing is a surprise. Under-promising here is why clients stay calm during setup week.

Day 0
Setup fee paid, 30-minute onboarding call. We collect everything from the list above. This is the only call they have to sit through.
Day 1–2
We build their WhatsApp business line and submit the business to Meta for verification. Nothing needed from them.
Day 2–4
Meta reviews every message format. This is the slow part and it's outside anyone's control — 24 to 48 hours per batch, sometimes a resubmission. This is exactly why we don't promise 48-hour activation.
Day 3–5
Google connected, menu and prices loaded, this week's diary moved in. The owner clicks Allow on one emailed link. Five minutes.
Day 5–7
Live. We email their Rebound number and a one-page card. The first reminders go out the same day.

The day it goes live — the three things they do

  1. We email their Rebound number. One number, in their salon's name. That's the whole product — no app to download, no password to remember.
  2. Save it in every staff phone as "Rebound". The owner's, the receptionist's, anyone who takes bookings.
  3. Each person sends "Hi" to it once. That's how we know their phone is allowed to book. They get a welcome message straight back with three example bookings to copy.
  4. Send the first real booking. Type it like a text message. A confirmation comes back in about five seconds — and that's it, they're running.
Then for the first two weeks we check every message before it reaches a customer, so nothing odd goes out while the system learns their services and their regulars' names. They'll see us approving in the background. After day 15 it runs on its own. Tell them this upfront — it turns "what if it makes a mistake?" into "oh, that's reassuring."

Who to spend your day on

walking away fast is the highest-paid skill you have

You have about 40 salon visits in you each month. Spending three of them on a business that will never buy is the most expensive mistake in this job.

✓ Good prospect — spend real time here
  • Salon or spa, appointment-based, not walk-in
  • 3.5–4.3★ on Google with 40–250 reviews — enough to care, room to improve
  • Owner is on the premises and answers for themselves
  • Average bill ₹500+ and a visible diary or register

  • They can tell you their no-show number without thinking

  • Under 25 appointments a week — the maths won't hold, they'll cancel by month three

The close

in this order — don't skip step 1
Six steps, ten minutes
  1. Show, don't tell. Send a booking into the demo number from your own phone, standing in front of them. Let them watch the reply come back. Ten seconds.
  2. Ask the two numbers. No-shows a week, average bill. Write the maths on paper and slide it across. Say nothing while they read it.
  3. Name the price and stop. "It's ₹7,999 a month plus ₹9,999 setup." Then stop talking. The next person to speak loses.
  4. Handle one objection properly — not three quickly. Ask "is it the price, or is it whether it'll work?" and answer the real one.
  5. Close on the calendar, not the contract. "I've got Thursday morning free for setup — shall I hold it for you?" It's far easier to say yes to a slot than to a signature.
  6. Collect the setup fee on the spot. UPI, in front of you. A payment promised for tomorrow is a payment made never.
You're not selling software. You're telling a salon owner how much money walked out of their door last month — and then handing them the way to stop it.
